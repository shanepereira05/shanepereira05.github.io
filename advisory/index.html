Syllabus

What a typical three months looks like.

Three months is tight on purpose. We scope hard in the first week to the two or three things that matter most for you, then run them end to end. What’s below is the full menu — not every box gets ticked, and that’s the point.

  1. Discovery & problem

    Making sure we’re solving the right thing, then moving fast to a prototype worth showing.

    Week one is about the real problem, not the described one. The rest of the month is about collapsing the distance from “we understand it” to “we have a thing in front of an operator.”

    • Operator interview protocol — watching the person work, not asking them to describe it
    • Problem statement rewritten in two paragraphs, without jargon
    • Using LLMs and MCP tooling to collapse discovery-to-prototype from weeks to days, concretely with your stack
    • Three things we’re explicitly not building this quarter, named out loud
  2. Measurement & prioritisation

    Closing the loop on what you ship, and building the mechanism that decides what comes next.

    The two gaps I see most often at seed and Series A. One stops you learning from what you shipped. The other means the roadmap is whoever shouted loudest last week.

    • Picking three outcome metrics (and killing the vanity ones)
    • Instrumentation audit — what’s not being measured that should be
    • Lightweight intake process for a 5–15 person product team
    • Opportunity cost framing — naming what gets deferred in the same paragraph as what gets built
  3. Team, cadence & renewal

    Making the next PM hire land, standing up habits that outlast the engagement, and deciding what’s next.

    The last month shifts from new material to durability. What keeps running after I stop coming to the calls. And whether we both want to keep going.

    • The role you actually have, written down — not the generic “PM” job description
    • Interview loop that screens for judgement over trivia, plus a 90-day ramp plan the new hire reads on day one
    • Weekly, monthly and quarterly rhythm, written down and defended
    • Review and decide: renew for another three months, pause, or wrap cleanly

The syllabus is a menu and a map, not a contract. We agree in week one which modules we’re actually doing and in what order. Renewals almost always add the modules we skipped.

How it works

The shape of a typical engagement.

Light-touch by design. Nothing about this is meant to feel like a second job for either of us.

  • CadenceTwo 45-minute calls a month, plus async over email or Slack as things come up. Written notes shared in a running doc that you own.
  • DurationThree months to start. Renewable in three-month blocks if we both want to keep going. Deliberately short so neither of us is stuck in a long contract that stopped working two months in.
  • CompensationEquity-first for seed and Series A (typically 0.1–0.25% vesting over 2 years with a 3-month cliff — the first engagement is the cliff, so it also functions as a trial for both sides). Small cash retainer for Series B and later, where the equity maths stops working.
  • ConfidentialityEverything you share stays with me. Nothing from your business ends up in my writing or talks without an explicit, written sign-off from you.
  • ScopeAdvice, framing, hard questions, the occasional spec review. I don’t do interim roles, don’t manage people, and don’t write code for you.
How to apply

Three questions, one email.

Instead of a form, I ask you to write me a short email. The answers tell me more about fit than any intake form would.

Please cover these, in any order:

  1. What are you building, in two short paragraphs. Who is it for, and what’s the current stage.
  2. What does an “advice from Shane” shaped problem look like for you right now. Be specific.
  3. Who else is advising you today. Names, or “no one yet,” both fine.
Write me the email

I read every one and reply within 7 days. If it’s not a fit I’ll usually suggest one or two other people who might be.

Common questions

Things people ask before writing in.

Why advisory and not fractional / interim PM?

I have a full-time job I enjoy. Fractional work would mean leaving it or doing it badly. Advisory is honest about the two-hour-a-month ceiling and prices that way.

What stage of company do you work best with?

Seed through Series B. Before seed you don’t need advisors yet, you need to go talk to users. After Series B you need people who can commit real hours, which I can’t.

Do you take cash-only engagements?

Only at Series B and later, where the equity maths stops making sense. For earlier stages, the equity grant is the entire point — it’s what keeps my incentives aligned with yours.

Can you introduce me to people in your network?

Yes, within reason and only after we’ve worked together long enough that I can vouch for what you’re doing. I treat intros as reputation currency and I spend it carefully.

Where are you based and what time zones do you work across?

Bangalore (IST). I regularly take calls with Europe (morning IST) and the US West Coast (late evening IST). US East Coast is harder but manageable.

Will this appear anywhere public?

I don’t publish a client list. If you’d like a public mention on your “Advisors” page I’ll usually say yes, but I won’t surface it from my end without your explicit ask.
